Go2: articulated RFT support and MPM checks

13 November 2026 · Geometry and previous low-speed investigation · Numerical summary

Support selection is implemented; material matching remains open

The new projected reaction solve holds a loaded foot and the full Go2 articulation without a backing floor. The explicit moving law remains the default. The extension selects compressive pressure and Coulomb friction near rest, and rewinds native solver history between trials.

No replacement policy has been trained. The current insertion-fit material still misses the stronger lateral MPM response. Historical walking-policy videos use their original solver and checkpoints.

Stationary Go2 supported only by RFT

This 10-second recording uses fixed joint PD targets, with no learned controller, rigid backing floor, or lower clipping plane. The field is an unbounded half-space whose surface is at 118 mm. Green arrows show interval-averaged granular body forces. All limbs remain coupled; each whole spherical foot has 128 parameter triangles and 4608 analytic integration nodes.

This isolates numerical support using nominal RFT strength, density 1600 kg/m³ and friction 0.4. It is not the calibrated 118 mm-deep MPM material. Four reaction iterations, a 0.20 m/s transition and 1.25 ms rigid steps are used. Final support is about 147.34 N, equal to robot weight.

The 0.02 m/s transition chatters. At 0.20 m/s, four and sixteen iterations settle within 0.34 mm in body height; halving the four-iteration timestep changes height by 0.32 mm. Foot motion reaches the sub-micrometre-per-second range. Zero residual on a flowing patch does not certify stable dynamics.

Holding and unloading

The isolated sphere has radius 22 mm, inertial mass 1 kg and independent downward load 18.42 N. The 118 mm layer has a separate rigid backing floor. A 0.373 strength multiplier matches a previous slow-insertion force fit. Starting both from 52.32 mm depth produces different settled depths: 106.37 mm in RFT and 79.73 mm in the fine MPM reference. RFT initialized at the settled MPM depth stops near 82.47 mm. Stationary support alone therefore does not establish equivalent loading history.

In the two-axis unloading comparison the foot is first held under load for one second, then pulled upward with 5 N. Both methods leave the bed; granular force vanishes after the whole sphere clears it. Compressive resistance on a buried upper surface is physically different from adhesion on a withdrawing lower surface. The flat-plate regression separately checks release, friction threshold and dissipation.

The two translational DOFs are vertical and forward; orientation is constrained. MPM is slowly inserted to 52.32 mm and released for one second before the sideways force starts. RFT begins near the previous settled MPM depth and is also held for one second. The coarse MPM and RFT depths and applied load are close when pushing begins; MPM retains its particle/material history. RFT has no compaction or ploughed-terrain history.

The 10 N case is a failed material comparison. RFT obtains most vertical support from the floor after sinking; that support is not credited to the granular model. Refining MPM cells from 10 to 5 mm changes displacement from 12.75 to 17.13 mm (34%) and the starting sole depth from 84.3 to 77.2 mm; the RFT trial starts near 84.0 mm. The reference is not yet grid converged or perfectly state matched. These short fixture comparisons do not certify whole-robot MPM convergence.

Cost at 4096 environments

The four-iteration profile measures 5,734 environment transitions/s, versus 21,347 for the corrected explicit profile: 3.72 times the control-step cost. GPU memory used is about 4.83 GiB.

At that measured rate, 1500 PPO iterations of 24 control steps need approximately 7.1 hours of simulation collection alone, before neural-network updates. MPM is used only for validation. Inner reaction trials account for this cost; moving-force evaluation itself remains about 11–21 ms on the common pose.

Implementation and validation

Newton revision e73147c247a5df0a366548aaaac709e882b9f1cf contains the reaction solve and native history rewind. The Isaac Lab adapter resets reaction warm starts only in selected environments. Material curriculum resets now preserve configured nominal density, friction, roughness and strength. CPU/GPU checks cover static holding, frictional slip, unloading dissipation, empty-layer rigid equivalence, selected-world reset and CUDA graph replay.

Passed: 33 focused Newton cases, 50 additional proxy regressions and 10 Isaac Lab cases. The new native clock/activation regression fails before the history fix and passes afterwards. These numerical checks do not resolve the material mismatch above.

Next: fit or revise the lateral/normal closure using the grid-checked MPM probes, validate the combined profile, then address collection cost before a 4096-world training run with reset material randomization and full commands through 1 m/s. G1 remains on hold.
