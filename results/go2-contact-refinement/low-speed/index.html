Go2 contact model validation

13 November 2026. This update follows the GM-Loco comparison. The feet retain 128 triangles over each complete sphere, with curved quadrature; lower-leg colliders remain coupled.

Fixed-link correction verified. New training has not started. Latest: articulated RFT reaction solve, support video and MPM loading checks. The newer mode passes stationary support checks; material matching remains open. G1 remains on hold.

Fixed foot/calf overlaps were adding internal forces

The foot sphere and calf cylinders are separate bodies connected by a fixed joint. Their overlapping internal surfaces were counted as exposed to sand. The correction groups enabled fixed links for containment checks, using each collider's owning-body frame. It preserves the collision shapes, triangle counts, masses and inertias.

The red dashed calf boundary lies inside the foot collider. It belongs to the interior of the fixed assembly.

In this prescribed buried pose, resting vertical support decreases from 186.72 to 132.70 N: 54.02 N was supplied by internal patches. This is a controlled geometry comparison, not a new policy result. The regression fails before the fix and passes after it for planar and curved integration. All 23 Newton RFT cases and six Isaac Lab geometry/native-coupling cases pass.

MPM provides sustained support, with measurable substrate influence

A 44 mm-diameter spherical foot is inserted slowly to 52.3 mm sole depth, held briefly, then released under an 18.42 N load. The probe has one vertical DOF and 1 kg inertial mass; load is applied independently. It isolates the foot, without a calf or robot controller. MPM uses density 1600 kg/m³, internal and interface friction 0.4, APIC transfer, zero cohesion/dilatancy and the stiff elastic limit.

The shallow 10 mm cases ran about ten seconds after release; the finer and deep-bed cases ran three seconds and reached negligible final speed. All five hold the load without rigid floor contact. The deep-bed delivered force balances the rigid foot's vertical momentum to a 95th-percentile force error of 1.4 micro-Newtons.

Increasing coupling passes changes depth by only 0.17 mm. Refining cells from 10 to 5 mm changes depth by 1.88 mm and the fitted insertion scale by about 9%. Doubling the MPM iteration cap from 200 to 400 on the 5 mm grid changes settled depth by about 0.08 mm and the insertion-fit scale from 0.376 to 0.373. These results provide a numerical reference with remaining grid uncertainty. The 236 mm bed produces greater settling, so clipping an RFT layer at its bottom does not reproduce confinement by itself.

A separate 36.83 N coarse-grid probe reaches 109.43 mm sole depth after three seconds and is still settling at 0.33 mm/s. Its 8.6 mm substrate clearance is smaller than one cell. It does not certify a converged nominal-load hold.

Equal material inputs have not matched the force response

For insertion from 15 to 50 mm depth, nominal published RFT needs a fitted strength multiplier of about 0.34–0.38 in the shallow MPM cases and 0.28 in the coarse deep bed. The finer shallow fit uses 0.376 with 4.7% normalized fitting error. This is a single-geometry, single-direction calibration result; it does not establish a universal coefficient. Tangential sliding, unloading, lower-leg geometry and further reference checks remain necessary before choosing the training distribution.

The explicit update causes a separate numerical sinking error

The RFT sphere starts at rest at an admissible depth. Explicit forces overshoot as velocity changes sign, causing repeated velocity reversals and net sinking. In a scalar diagnostic, selecting a reaction inside the two directional RFT limits satisfies momentum balance and holds an admissible load.

The implicit 1D case has zero drift and zero positive granular work in these holds at four and eight substeps. It uses an unbounded bed. The 118 mm line is only a reference. It is not an articulated solver or an MPM validation.

Implementation and training status

The fixed-link boundary bug is corrected and regression-tested. The newer projected articulated reaction mode passes tested stationary support checks. Material matching remains open. No replacement walking policy has been trained.

The next policy study remains one 4096-environment run, full forward commands through 1.0 m/s, reset randomization and a material-only curriculum. Physics acceptance comes first. Short observation history and wider calibrated material variation follow that acceptance.
