Go2 contact-resolution follow-up

Original policy comparison · Methods and reproduction

Geometry and numerical implementation complete. New training has not started. The long-hold fixture still creeps or reaches the rigid floor. Stable near-rest reaction selection and material matching remain required before another training run. G1 work is paused.

Contact geometry

The entire spherical foot has 128 triangles, including 64 on its lower hemisphere. The two lower calf cylinders have 128 and 96 triangles each. The upper calf, thighs, hips and torso keep their previous resolution. All selected leg and thigh surfaces remain coupled.

Collision dimensions, poses, mass and inertia are preserved. The robot has 2,032 retained integration triangles, compared with 1,536 previously. Flat surface subdivision remains separate from curved-surface accuracy.

The selected rule uses 36 Gauss points per foot triangle and nine per refined calf triangle. These points lie on the analytic curved collider and use its true normal and area Jacobian. Shared templates avoid duplicating point geometry across 4,096 environments. Triangle count and integration-point count are distinct.

Accuracy on recorded walking motion

Selected foot integration: 0.51% pooled force and 0.94% pooled torque difference from the reference. Individual outliers remain; these are numerical differences, not material-model accuracy.

Both rigid-control recordings last ten seconds, with commands 0.5 and 1.0 m/s. The audit selects 32 frames from each and uses the exact recorded world-space COM twists. It crosses four heights (3, 30, 60, 118 mm), two bulk-friction values and two interface ratios. Density is 1,800 kg/m³ and strength multiplier 1.2. The same poses, collider unions and material cases are used for every candidate.

Union rejection covers only colliders of the same body; it does not resolve shielding between articulated links.

Selected calf integration has 0.82% pooled force and 0.62% pooled torque difference. Its maximum normalized differences are 8.2% and 17.1%.

The worst material-case calf force percentile is about 3.2%, above the proposed 2% target. The profile therefore remains a candidate. Foot percentile accuracy improves, while strict pointwise accuracy, calf resolution, cost and static support still require work.

Cost at 4,096 environments

Both profiles run through the native Go2 environment on one RTX PRO 4500 GPU. The direct RFT timings use identical recorded poses and twists at two immersion offsets. The end-to-end measurements step zero actions and can follow different trajectories; they exclude PPO updates.

Measured transitions per second: previous 60,929; refined 33,611. Reported total GPU use is 3.86 GiB, including the CUDA context and other processes.

Static support remains unresolved

A vertical prismatic fixture applies 18.42, 36.83 and 55.25 N to separate 1 kg intruders. The nominal load is one quarter of Go2's weight. Each foot uses the new 128-triangle curved integration, in a 118 mm layer, for ten seconds at three timesteps. The two supported loads start at rest at a depth with 110% of the required bearing capacity, independently checked against the analytic sphere integral. This removes landing impact from the support test.

The nominal-load foot reaches the rigid substrate. The half-load foot still creeps more than the proposed 2 mm allowance from two to ten seconds. The overload is retained as a diagnostic. No matched Go2 MPM hold reference is claimed here. More quadrature does not resolve the explicit near-rest reaction selection.

The next solver stage is a bounded near-rest reaction treatment coupled to the articulated dynamics, followed by prolonged holds, load/unload work, timestep convergence and MPM force matching. Reset randomization and new Go2 training follow those gates. The existing videos retain their original physics and policy weights.

Verification and records
