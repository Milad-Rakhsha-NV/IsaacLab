RFT foot resolution: G1 and Go2

Numerical geometry and force-integration study. Existing training defaults and checkpoints remain coarse.

Foot surface integration

Counts below refer to one foot. The current training mesh has 2 triangles on the sole and 12 over the complete foot collision box. Torso and leg triangles do not measure sole resolution. The flat box geometry is exact, but force integration can still need refinement when rotation changes depth, velocity direction or activation across a face. RFT integrates each triangle at three quadrature points after clipping it against the material layer.

The audit prescribes 240 recorded poses with exact COM twists from the final policy at both commands, swept over 41 material/height combinations. Each foot is evaluated independently; a parity check reproduces the original full-model foot wrenches. Errors below are pooled L2 differences against a 10 mm maximum-edge reference. Sole-only reactions exclude side and upper faces; whole-foot reactions include them. Moments are about the actual foot body COM. This checks numerical quadrature, not material validity.

Go2: curved foot resolution

The Go2 collider is a sphere of radius 22 mm. Its current UV tessellation has edges up to 15.6 mm. Curved feet require accurate surface normals as well as enough integration points. Subdividing existing flat facets retains their coarse curvature. Regenerating the sphere, or using a uniform icosphere mesh, improves both its shape and force integration. These RFT surfaces can be refined independently of the rigid/MPM collision geometry and robot inertial properties.

This preliminary test prescribes 150 centered-sphere states at four friction/interface combinations: 1–44 mm penetration, insertion, translation, withdrawal and 8 rad/s rotation at three orientations. The corrected force law is used, with moments about the sphere center. The numerical reference has 384 UV segments (about 0.51 mm edges). It is a surface self-convergence study, rather than robot-policy or physical-material validation.

The diagrams classify lower-half faces by their centroids at the nominal orientation; the submerged leading patch changes during motion.

A 1,280-triangle uniform sphere is an economical candidate, with 5,120 triangles as a stricter comparison. Actual recorded foot motion, per-depth errors and throughput at 4,096 environments must determine the training resolution. Refinement should target foot colliders and be cached at model construction. Existing task defaults and checkpoints still use the coarse surfaces. New policies require retraining after the resolution is selected. The separate near-rest support failure remains unresolved.
