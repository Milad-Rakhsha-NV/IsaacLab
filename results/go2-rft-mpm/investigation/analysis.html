Rate and material history

Speed changes hold the maximum penetration fixed at 40 mm. MPM includes inertia and evolving deformation; the RFT training closure has no inertial term and uses a fixed free surface. Its 0.04 m/s regularization and experimental bearing term still affect near-rest forces. The probe is held kinematically, without an imposed robot-weight load. Rate differences can include numerical effects.

During the final part of the fixed-depth hold, this prescribed-foot test gives -0.019 N from MPM versus 13.77 N from the nominal RFT bearing closure. The models therefore also differ in their near-rest response.

Parameter correspondence

Density, initial depth and the internal friction invariant agree as inputs. A direct constitutive check gives τ = 399.99997 Pa at P = 1,000 Pa and μ = 0.4, using τ = √(s:s/2). MPM collider friction is μ times the same surface/internal ratio used by RFT. This establishes the meaning of μ, rather than equivalence of the complete material response.

The MPM bed uses E = 10¹⁵ Pa, ν = 0.3, compressive cap 10¹⁵ Pa, zero cohesion/tensile strength, viscosity, hardening and dilatancy. These settings approximate a stiff frictional continuum. RFT has no equivalent elastic stiffness, stress history or packing state; its 0.8–1.2 strength factor and bearing closure have no direct MPM input counterpart.

Frozen-policy material comparison

Both policies are evaluated for 3 s, with the same 0.50 m/s forward command and seed 123. These are short parameter-sensitivity trials, not success-rate estimates. Lower settings: 1,400 kg/m³, μ=0.3, ratio=0.6, depth=60 mm. Nominal: 1,600, 0.4, 1.0, 118 mm. Higher settings: 1,800, 0.5, 0.8, 100 mm. Each lies within the RFT reset-randomization ranges; three selected settings do not cover their full joint distribution. The RFT comparison uses strength factor 1.0.

The frozen rigid policy was trained for 600 iterations and the RFT policy for 1,500, both with 4,096 environments and one training seed. These different budgets prevent a controlled causal attribution to material training alone.

Coupling audit

Multi-pass solves now restore the entire persistent entry state before re-solving the same interval. A native MPM/APIC regression failed before this correction and passes afterward. Preparation also retains the entry material history. All cases below use the corrected implementation; the four original one-pass, unprepared videos are retained as the original exploratory recordings.

Relaxation α blends new proxy feedback with its previous value. At α=0.1, the recurrence is 0.9 × previous + 0.1 × new; the steady force is not simply multiplied by 0.1. Changing timestep also changes this temporal filter. The sweep changes relaxation, pass count and substeps with an otherwise fixed policy and a 2 m prepared bed.

Interpretation

These are simulation comparisons, without measured physical-sand data. The current robot test uses a shallow sand layer over a rigid floor. Whole-robot momentum audits quantify granular and backing-floor support; the rigid-floor control validates the load-accounting method.

The RFT-trained policy handles this MPM setup better than the rigid-trained policy in the selected tests. The evidence supports useful training exposure to granular resistance. It does not yet show that the two material models represent the same sand. A quantitative transfer benchmark needs a converged MPM contact/transfer setup, then independent force calibration and longer multi-seed policy evaluations.
